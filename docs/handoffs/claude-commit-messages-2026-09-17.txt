COMMIT 6d14e26
================================================================
Take the profile photo off both receipts, and name the currency

No photograph on the receipt, and none fetched for it. The counterparty's
server-held photo was drawn on the in-app receipt and on the shared PNG,
with a brand-gradient disc where they had none.

Both are gone, and the fallback is the reason the photo could not simply
be made optional: where there was no picture the largest mark on the panel
carried nothing at all, and was there only because the layout had a hole
shaped like a face. A receipt is a record that gets forwarded — to a chat,
to an accountant, to whoever asks — and a face is the one thing on it that
is not a fact about the payment.

The stronger half is that the REQUEST is gone too. Sharing a receipt used
to call loadCounterpartyPhoto, a request to the server for the other
party's picture, purely to feed the avatar. Not making it is the point.
profileAvatar.jsx stays; the scan card and the dashboard still use it.

With the disc removed the shared image's counterparty block centres under
the amount instead of sitting beside a circle, and its height is measured
from its content rather than fixed at the height of an avatar.

Also: the in-app hero now names its currency. fmtMoney draws the symbol,
and a symbol is not the currency — a rupee sign is shared by five
countries and a dollar sign by more than twenty. On a cross-border receipt
"1,106.61₹" alone does not say which rupee left the account. The shared
PNG has carried that line since it was written; the screen it is a picture
of did not, so the two disagreed about how completely the same payment was
described. Suppressed where fmtMoney already ends in the code.

Tests: the photo suites asserted the opposite and are rewritten to their
inverse rather than deleted — no photo reaches the model whatever is
passed, the module never asks the server for one, and the seeded photo's
colour appears in zero pixels of the shared PNG. coin-receipt's lone-tab
assertion is unchanged in substance.

COMMIT 8394bd7
================================================================
Put the GH2H mark in the QR's fourth corner

Three corners of a QR carry finder patterns; the fourth carried nothing.
The mark from the Send and Receive tiles goes there, cycling G / H / 2 / H
on the same two-second tick as the centre badge.

Where it sits was measured. Centred on the symbol's own bottom-right 7x7
block -- the position that mirrors a finder exactly -- it lands on the
version 5 alignment pattern, which a decoder reads to correct perspective.
Covering that costs localisation, not error-correction capacity: across 192
rasters (8 badge colours x 6 module scales x 4 blur levels) the bare code
decoded 176 times, the mark on the block 138, and a solid dark mark on the
block only 73.

Moved two modules out, so no alignment module falls under it and the
overhang goes into the quiet zone instead, the same 7-module circle decoded
184/192 -- no worse than the code with no mark on it. That is also why the
mark is mostly white: a light badge reads as a light region, a dark one
merges with the modules around it.

The four tile colours go through the same darkener the centre disc uses.
They were drawn to sit on white, not to be read against it -- #3B82F6
measures 2.03:1 -- and here the ring and the letter ARE the mark, on white.

Tests: the rasteriser now models both badges; every face decodes at every
badge colour; the alignment pattern's clearance is asserted in module
coordinates; and the letters and colours are read back out of
flipIcons.jsx, so the corner and the tile cannot drift apart.

COMMIT 3c11700
================================================================
Rebuild the shared receipt as N3, and share the GH2H mark

The receipt image was a white card with a purple strip across the top, and
the headline, the status tick and the amount all competed for the same
middle. N3 inverts it: the whole card is the brand gradient and carries
exactly one thing -- what happened and how much -- and every fact somebody
reads back later sits on a white panel floating inside it. The purple is
what survives at thumbnail size in a chat; the white panel is the part that
gets forwarded to an accountant. Nothing appears in both.

Two things the layout now does that it did not:

The Transaction ID has its own block instead of a right-aligned detail row.
It is twelve coloured symbols rather than a word, and squeezed into a row it
shrank until it could not be read off a screenshot.

The Creator Share is on every receipt, as a chip on the purple, INCLUDING at
zero. A chip that vanishes at 0% leaves a reader to assume a share was taken
and not shown; "0% * 0.00 INR" is the only version of that which is true. At
zero it is drawn quieter, not hidden.

That chip also fixed a real defect it exposed: a Creator Share receipt was
labelled "Money received", which with the chip present reads as an ordinary
payment carrying a share of exactly its own size. heroLabel now says Creator
Share, and the chip drops to the rate alone, because on that receipt the
figure above IS the share amount. Both decisions are in the model, not the
renderer, so the tests can read them.

The perforation notches are filled with the card's own gradient object
rather than a stand-in purple, so they match at whatever height the card
ends up.

Separately: GH2H_LETTERS and GH2H_LETTER_COLORS move to module scope in
flipIcons.jsx. They were local to GH2HFlipCircle, which was fine while the
Send and Receive tiles were the only thing drawing them; the QR's fourth
corner draws the same mark, and it now reads those arrays instead of
carrying a second copy. flipIcons.jsx is emitted first in FRONTEND_MODULES,
which matters -- var initialisers do not hoist across the bundle.

Verified: 22 receipt-image tests, 17 QR tests, 88 across the four suites
that touch flipIcons. The eight receipt suites fail 15 with and without
these changes -- identical names both runs -- so all 15 are pre-existing
(flag CDN and short-link).

COMMIT 7c3be02
================================================================
Rebuild N3's panel as flat rows, to the approved design

The panel led with a large centred name and gave the conversion a filled box
of its own. Both pulled the eye away from the figure on the purple, which is
the one thing this card exists to show. Every fact is a label/value row now,
the conversion among them rather than announcing itself as a separate event,
and Date and Time share one row.

The counterparty's country moves into the To row as a flag chip at the right
edge. The name is measured to stop short of it rather than the flag being
pushed past the panel, which is how a long name loses its country.

The Transaction ID block is outlined instead of filled: on a white panel a
filled block reads as a second card. It stays a block rather than a row --
twelve coloured symbols right-aligned in a row shrank until they could not be
read off a screenshot.

The hero figure takes a MINUS when money left, and nothing when it arrived.
A sign rather than a colour: the figure is white on the brand gradient, where
red-for-out/green-for-in would either fight that ground or vanish into it,
and a minus survives greyscale, a screenshot and a colour-blind reader. No
"+" on the received side -- a leading plus reads as an adjustment or a credit
note rather than as money arriving. amountText stays unsigned; the sign is a
new heroAmountText, decided in the model so the renderer still decides
nothing.

One thing in the design was NOT copied. It showed the rate as
"1 EUR = 91.80 INR" where the server had recorded the other direction.
Printing that means inverting a number and calling it a record, so the rate
keeps its stored direction -- see ReceiptModal's fxRateLabel.

The flag row was verified by serving flagcdn locally from the render rig.
The sandbox cannot reach that host, so without it the flag silently would not
draw and the row carrying it would have shipped unrendered.

The eight receipt suites fail 15 with and without this change -- identical
names both runs -- so all 15 remain pre-existing.

COMMIT 27ae4ed
================================================================
Shrink the QR's corner mark, and draw the reference at its real length

The corner mark was a 7-module circle -- exactly a finder pattern's size --
and read as a fourth finder rather than as a mark. It is 2.8 modules of
radius now, 64% of that area.

Anchoring changed with it. The badge is positioned by its OUTER edge instead
of its centre, because the overhang past the symbol is what sets it in the
corner and resizing it should not slide it diagonally away. That also means
shrinking it can only INCREASE its clearance from the version 5 alignment
pattern, so a future size change cannot quietly reintroduce the overlap that
cost 138/192 when this started. Measured: 3.1, 2.8, 2.5 and 2.2 all decode
176/192, which is the bare code's own rate.

Separately, a fixture bug worth naming. The render rig used a 12-symbol
transaction reference -- a Gloobal ID's length, not a transaction ID's, which
is 20 (TXN_ID_LENGTH in backend/utils/idGenerators.js). Every picture of the
receipt I have looked at drew that block at 60% of its real content, so the
one row whose whole justification is that it must stay readable was the one
row never seen at full length. It fits; the symbols shrink slightly and stay
legible.

The guard reads TXN_ID_LENGTH out of the generator rather than restating 20,
checks the fixture against it, and checks the model neither truncates nor
ellipsises a full-length reference -- including one that arrives grouped with
spaces, as rows saved before that rule was settled still do.

COMMIT b51b68e
================================================================
One share, carrying the receipt and the link

The receipt had two share buttons: the amount card sent the picture, and the
Transaction ID box sent the summary and the /t/ link. Whichever you pressed,
you did not send the other half -- and neither half is the receipt on its
own. A picture cannot be clicked through to the transaction; a link is not
something you can look at in a chat.

They are one action now. The Share button builds a single payload with the
PNG, the summary and the link, and the second button is gone. Copying the
Transaction ID is still its own button: copying is not sharing.

The awkward part is receiptShareAttempts, and it exists because
navigator.share is not uniform. A target that accepts files does not
necessarily accept `url` beside them, and the whole payload is rejected when
any part is unsupported -- so asking for everything and giving up on a false
from canShare would lose the picture on exactly the platforms that can show
it. Each rung drops the least valuable thing that could be causing the
refusal, canShare decides rather than a browser sniff, and every rung keeps
the picture:

  1. picture + summary + link
  2. the same with the link folded into `text`, the field that survives
  3. picture and summary alone -- reported as its own outcome, so the caller
     says the link did not go instead of dropping it silently

A rejection from navigator.share itself stops the ladder: that is the
sheet's answer, not the payload's, and retrying would put a second sheet in
front of someone who just closed one. Where files cannot be shared at all,
the picture downloads and the link goes to the clipboard in the same
gesture -- one action, both halves, by whichever route the platform allows.

Two bugs fixed on the way.

The summary is now built from the TAB's receipt. The picture always was;
this text was not, so sharing a payment's Creator Share tab sent a picture
of the share captioned with the payment's figure.

tests/receipt-link-share.test.mjs had been failing on all six of its browser
tests, and not because of this: it waited on aria-label "Share transaction",
renamed to "Share receipt link" some time ago. It now drives the single
button and asserts what only a browser can -- that one tap produces one
navigator.share call carrying a real >5KB PNG and the /t/ code together.

The old guard "the share sends only the image, with no text" is replaced
rather than deleted. Its actual concern was the tagline not being repeated
in the share sheet, and that assertion is kept.

Receipt suites: 15 failures before, 8 after, and the 8 are a strict subset
of the 15 -- no new failures, six link-share tests fixed.
